Getting office refurbishment costs wrong can derail a project before it starts. Budget too low and you're cutting corners mid-build. Budget too high and you've tied up capital that could be working elsewhere.

The challenge is that refurbishment costs vary enormously depending on specification, building condition, and location. This guide walks through how to calculate your budget accurately, what drives costs up or down, and how to get a fixed-price quote you can rely on.

## What Office Refurbishment Costs Cover for UK Businesses

Office refurbishment costs in the UK typically range from £40 to £200 or more per square foot. Where you land within that range depends on three things: your specification grade, regional labour rates, and how much work the space actually requires.

A refurbishment works with an existing finished space. You're updating, improving, or reconfiguring what's already there. A fit out, on the other hand, starts from a shell or Cat A condition and builds everything fresh.

Most refurbishment contracts include:

- **Partitions and layout changes:** new walls, doors, and reconfigured floor plates
- **Finishes:** flooring, decoration, ceilings, and feature walls
- **M&E upgrades:** lighting, power, HVAC improvements, and fire alarm modifications
- **Compliance works:** fire stopping, accessibility improvements, and Building Regulations sign-off

Furniture, AV equipment, IT infrastructure, and professional fees typically sit outside the main contract. They add to your total budget, so it's worth accounting for them from the start.

## Average Office Refurbishment Cost per Square Foot in the UK

Costs vary significantly based on specification level and location. A straightforward cosmetic refresh sits at the lower end of the scale. A premium refurbishment with bespoke joinery and full M&E replacement sits at the top.

| Specification Level | What's Included | Typical Use Case |
|---------------------|-----------------|------------------|
| Low | Redecoration, new carpets, minor repairs | Short-term leases, budget-conscious projects |
| Medium | New partitions, upgraded lighting, improved M&E | Most commercial refurbishments |
| High | Bespoke joinery, premium finishes, full M&E replacement | Corporate headquarters, client-facing spaces |

London commands a premium over regional locations, sometimes 20% to 40% higher. Access constraints, labour rates, and logistics all contribute. Cities like Manchester, Birmingham, and Bristol typically fall somewhere between London and smaller regional centres.

## How To Calculate Your Total Office Refurbishment Budget

Working out your budget involves more than multiplying floor area by a cost per square foot. The following approach accounts for variables that most businesses overlook.

### Step 1. Confirm Your Net Internal Floor Area

Start with your net internal area (NIA), not the gross internal area (GIA). NIA measures usable floor space and excludes cores, risers, and common areas. This figure gives you a more accurate basis for cost calculations.

Your landlord or managing agent can usually provide measured floor plans. Alternatively, a quick site measure by your contractor will confirm the numbers.

### Step 2. Select a Specification Tier

Your specification choice has the biggest impact on cost. A cosmetic refresh for a short lease looks very different from a premium refurbishment designed to last a decade.

Consider your lease length, brand requirements, and how the space will be used. Client-facing areas often justify higher specifications. Back-of-house spaces can be more functional.

### Step 3. Apply a Realistic Contingency

Contingency covers the unexpected. Hidden services, asbestos, or structural issues often only emerge once work begins.

For newer buildings in good condition, 5% to 10% is often sufficient. Older premises with unknown M&E may warrant 10% to 15%.

### Step 4. Add Excluded Items and Professional Fees

Several items sit outside your main contract:

- **Professional fees:** architects, project managers, CDM advisors (typically 10% to 15% of construction cost)
- **FF&E:** desks, chairs, storage, and soft furnishings
- **IT and AV:** cabling, data infrastructure, screens, and conferencing equipment
- **Dilapidations:** end-of-lease reinstatement costs if you're refurbishing before moving out

## Low, Medium and High Specification Refurbishment Costs

Each specification tier delivers something different. Matching your budget to your business objectives starts with understanding what you're actually getting.

### Low Specification Refurbishment

Low specification covers cosmetic works only. Redecoration, new carpet tiles, and basic repairs. The space will look refreshed, though the underlying infrastructure remains unchanged.

This tier suits short leases or situations where budget constraints outweigh long-term considerations.

### Medium Specification Refurbishment

Most commercial refurbishments fall into this category. New partitions, suspended ceilings, upgraded lighting, and improved small power distribution. The result is a functional, modern workspace that serves most business requirements well.

### High Specification Refurbishment

Premium finishes, bespoke joinery, feature lighting, and full M&E replacement characterise this tier. It's appropriate for corporate headquarters, client-facing environments, or businesses planning to occupy the space for an extended period.

## Key Factors That Affect Office Refurbishment Costs

Several variables move the price up or down. Understanding them helps you anticipate where costs might increase or where savings might be found.

### Scope and Structural Works

A cosmetic refresh costs considerably less than structural alterations. Opening up floor plates, relocating cores, or creating new openings through walls adds complexity, time, and cost.

### Building Age and Existing M&E Condition

Older buildings often require rewiring, upgraded mechanical systems, or asbestos removal. These works aren't always visible during initial surveys, which is why contingency matters.

### Programme Length and Out-of-Hours Working

Compressed programmes or weekend-only access increase labour costs. However, out-of-hours working reduces disruption to your operations. Many businesses find this trade-off worthwhile.

### Landlord and Lease Requirements

Some landlords specify approved contractors, require enhanced specifications, or impose restrictions on working hours. Licence for alterations approvals can also affect programme and cost.

## Cat A and Cat B Office Refurbishment Costs Explained

These terms appear frequently in commercial property. Understanding the distinction helps when budgeting.

- **Cat A:** the landlord's base build. Raised floors, suspended ceilings, basic M&E distribution, and a neutral finish ready for tenant fit out.
- **Cat B:** the occupier's fit out. Partitions, finishes, branding, furniture, and everything that makes the space functional for your business.

Most refurbishments are Cat B works. However, some projects involve upgrading or replacing Cat A elements that have reached the end of their useful life. See our [Cat A vs Cat B fit out guide](/insights/cat-a-vs-cat-b-fit-out/) for the full breakdown.

## London and Regional UK Office Refurbishment Cost Differences

London typically commands a 20% to 40% premium over regional locations. Access constraints in central London, restricted delivery times, limited storage, and complex logistics all contribute to higher costs.

Regional cities offer more competitive rates, though major centres like Manchester and Birmingham have seen costs rise as demand increases. Smaller towns and out-of-town locations generally offer the most competitive pricing.

## Compliance and Regulatory Costs To Budget For

Compliance isn't optional. The associated costs are often underestimated.

### CDM 2015 Duties

The Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 apply to most commercial refurbishment projects. You'll typically require a Principal Designer and Principal Contractor, with associated fees and responsibilities.

### Fire Safety and Building Regulations

Fire stopping, compartmentation, emergency lighting, and signage all require attention. Building control approval, either through your local authority or an approved inspector, adds time and cost but ensures your completed space is compliant.

### Accessibility and Part M Requirements

Building Regulations Part M covers accessibility. Depending on your scope, you may have to address accessible WCs, door widths, and circulation routes.

## How Much Contingency To Allow in Your Refurbishment Budget

Contingency protects you from cost overruns when unexpected issues arise. The right amount depends on your building's age and condition.

For newer buildings with well-documented M&E systems, 5% to 10% is often adequate. Older premises, particularly those with limited survey information, may warrant 10% to 15% or more.
