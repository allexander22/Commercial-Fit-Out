Office refurbishments don't have to mean weeks of noise, dust, and disruption for your team. When construction work happens outside business hours, your staff arrive each morning to a clean workspace while the project progresses overnight.

This guide covers the practical benefits of out of hours working, from protecting productivity and maintaining compliance to compressing project timelines and achieving fixed-price certainty.

## What out of hours working means in a commercial fit out

Scheduling refurbishment work outside normal business hours keeps your office running without interruption, protects your team from construction hazards, and lets contractors work faster. When the building is empty, there's no need to work around desks, meetings, or people trying to concentrate. The result is a quicker project with far less disruption to daily operations.

Out of hours working refers to any construction activity that happens when your office isn't occupied. For most businesses, that means evenings after 6pm, weekends, and bank holidays. Some projects combine all three to compress timelines significantly.

### Evening and night shift working

Evening shifts typically start once your team has left for the day. Contractors arrive around 6pm and work through until midnight or later, depending on building access agreements and local noise restrictions.

This window suits trades that generate noise or dust. Electrical first fix, mechanical installations, partition walls, and decoration can all progress without anyone trying to work nearby. By morning, the site is cleared and ready for your staff to return.

Night shifts extend further into the early hours. For offices operating standard 9-to-5 hours, night working creates a full construction window while leaving the space clean and accessible each morning.

### Weekend and bank holiday working

Two full days of uninterrupted access each weekend adds up quickly. A project that might take six weeks with weekday-only access can often complete in four weeks when weekends are included.

Retail and healthcare environments particularly benefit from weekend scheduling. Both typically have extended weekday operating hours that limit daytime construction access, making weekends the most practical option for significant fit out work.

### Phased access to occupied buildings

Larger refurbishments often use phased access, where work progresses through the building in sections while other areas remain operational. You might refurbish one floor at a time, or divide an open-plan office into zones.

Staff relocate temporarily within the building as each phase completes. This approach requires careful coordination, but it means the business never stops operating entirely. Phased programmes work well when combined with evening and weekend shifts to accelerate each zone's completion.

## Minimise disruption to daily business operations

For most businesses, avoiding disruption is the primary reason to choose out of hours refurbishment. When contractors work while the office is empty, your team arrives each morning to a clean, quiet workspace rather than navigating around ladders, dust sheets, and power tools.

The difference is significant. Daytime construction means:

- **Constant noise:** drilling, cutting, and hammering while people try to concentrate
- **Dust and debris:** particles settling on desks, equipment, and personal belongings
- **Restricted access:** meeting rooms cordoned off, toilets out of service, corridors blocked
- **Client disruption:** visitors seeing an active construction site rather than a professional environment

Out of hours working removes all of this. Your team works normally during the day. Contractors work when the building is empty. The two activities never overlap.

For businesses that rely on client meetings or have customer-facing operations, maintaining a professional environment matters enormously. A chaotic construction site can undermine confidence in your organisation, even if the disruption is temporary.

## Protect staff productivity and morale

Beyond practical disruptions, there's a human element worth considering. Staff working alongside construction activity often report difficulty concentrating, frustration with noise and mess, and general dissatisfaction with their working environment.

When your team arrives each morning to a clean, work-ready space, they can focus on their jobs rather than the refurbishment happening around them. Normal productivity levels continue throughout the project, and there's no gradual erosion of morale as the weeks drag on.

Prolonged exposure to construction activity also tends to increase complaints to facilities managers and HR teams. People become frustrated, and that frustration gets directed somewhere. Out of hours working removes this friction entirely. Staff may barely notice the refurbishment is happening until they see the finished result.

## Improve health and safety on the refurbishment site

Separating office workers from active construction reduces risk for everyone involved. Under CDM 2015, the Construction Design and Management Regulations that govern UK construction projects, there are clear obligations around managing site access and protecting people who aren't part of the construction team.

When contractors have exclusive access to the building, they can implement proper site segregation without compromise. Dust containment works more effectively. Fire escape routes stay clear. There's no risk of someone wandering into an active work zone.

The safety benefits include:

- **Reduced pedestrian traffic:** fewer people near active work zones means fewer opportunities for accidents
- **Clearer fire escape routes:** no conflict between construction materials and evacuation paths during occupied hours
- **Better dust and fume control:** extraction and containment measures work properly without occupied areas nearby
- **Simpler site management:** contractors can focus on the work rather than managing interactions with office staff

For facilities managers responsible for building safety, out of hours working simplifies compliance significantly. The separation between construction activity and normal office operations is clean and clear.

## Deliver the office refurbishment faster

Working evenings, nights, and weekends compresses the overall programme considerably. Contractors can begin immediately rather than waiting for the office to empty each day, and they can work continuously without pausing for meetings or accommodating office activities.

Consider a straightforward example. A refurbishment that might take six weeks with daytime-only access could potentially complete in four weeks with evening and weekend working added. The total hours of construction activity remain similar, but the calendar time shrinks.

Faster completion means your business benefits from the refurbished space sooner. For organisations planning around lease events, expansion, or operational changes, this compressed timeline can be valuable. It also reduces the total period during which the project is ongoing, even if the day-to-day disruption is minimal.

| Working Pattern | Typical Weekly Hours | Calendar Impact |

| Daytime only (Mon-Fri) | 40-45 hours | Standard programme |
| Evenings added (Mon-Fri) | 60-70 hours | 30-40% faster |
| Evenings plus weekends | 80-90 hours | 40-50% faster |

The exact impact depends on the scope of work and access arrangements, but the principle holds. More working hours per week means fewer weeks overall.

## Maintain compliance with CDM 2015 and Building Regulations

Out of hours scheduling supports regulatory compliance in several practical ways. When the building is unoccupied, controlling site access becomes straightforward. Dust containment works properly. Fire routes stay clear without constant monitoring.

CDM 2015 places specific duties on clients, designers, and contractors to manage health and safety risks throughout construction projects. One key requirement involves separating construction activity from other building users. Out of hours working achieves this separation naturally, without the need for complex temporary arrangements.

Building Regulations compliance also benefits from uninterrupted working. Inspections can be scheduled more flexibly, and contractors can complete work in logical sequences without pausing to accommodate office operations. Electrical testing, fire alarm commissioning, and mechanical system checks all proceed more smoothly when the building is empty.

For multi-tenanted buildings, out of hours working often satisfies building management requirements around noise, dust, and access. Many commercial landlords prefer or require refurbishment work to happen outside core business hours to protect other tenants.
